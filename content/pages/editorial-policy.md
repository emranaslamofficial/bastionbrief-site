## Our purpose

Bastion Brief publishes cybersecurity education for a general audience. Our goal is to help readers recognise threats, protect their accounts, devices and data, and respond calmly when something goes wrong. Everything we publish is written from a defensive perspective.

## The safety rule

We explain how attacks work only at the level a defender needs. We do not publish:

- step-by-step instructions for accessing accounts, devices or systems without authorisation;
- methods for stealing credentials, session data or verification codes;
- instructions for creating, obtaining or deploying malware;
- guidance for running phishing or social engineering campaigns;
- techniques for bypassing authentication or two-factor authentication.

When a reader's question is phrased in an offensive way — for example, "how to hack an Instagram account" — we answer the defensive question behind it: how these attacks happen, how to recognise them and how to stop them.

Articles about ethical hacking focus on authorised testing, legal and ethical boundaries, professional process and legal practice environments.

## Research and sources

We base our articles on primary and authoritative sources wherever possible, including:

- official help centres and security documentation from platforms and vendors;
- standards bodies such as NIST, the IETF, OWASP and the FIDO Alliance;
- national cybersecurity and consumer protection agencies, such as CISA, the FTC and the UK NCSC;
- published research from established security organisations.

Articles include a "Sources and further reading" section linking to key references. We link to sources so readers can verify our claims and go deeper.

## What we do not do

- **We do not invent statistics.** If we cannot cite a figure from a credible source, we do not use one.
- **We do not fabricate quotes, case studies, testimonials or credentials.** Illustrative scenarios are clearly labelled as such.
- **We do not present opinion as fact.** Where experts disagree or evidence is limited, we say so.
- **We do not recommend specific commercial products in exchange for payment.**

## Accuracy and updates

Platforms change their settings, menus and recovery processes frequently, and security guidance evolves. We review articles periodically and when we become aware of significant changes.

Each article displays its original publication date. When an article is meaningfully revised — for example, to reflect new settings or updated guidance — we also display an "Updated" date. Minor edits such as typo fixes do not change the updated date.

## Corrections

If you believe something in an article is inaccurate, out of date or unclear, please tell us through the [contact page](/contact/). Include the article and, if possible, a source. We review every correction request. Where we agree an error was made, we fix it promptly and, for significant errors, add a note to the article explaining what changed.

## Independence and funding

Bastion Brief does not run third-party advertising trackers. If the site introduces advertising, sponsorships or affiliate links in the future, they will be clearly disclosed on the pages where they appear and described in this policy. Commercial relationships will never determine what we recommend.

## Authorship

Articles are published under the Bastion Brief Editorial Team byline, and the team takes collective responsibility for their accuracy. If individual authors or reviewers are credited in future, their profiles will describe only real, verifiable experience and qualifications.

## Use of automated tools

Writing and research tools may be used to assist with drafting, editing and checking. Every published article is reviewed against its sources by the editorial team before publication, and the team is responsible for its content.
