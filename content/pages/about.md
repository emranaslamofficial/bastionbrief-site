## Why we started

Most people who lose an account to a scammer are not careless. They are busy, they are trusting, and they were targeted by someone who does this full-time. Meanwhile, much of the security advice online is either written for professionals, buried in jargon, or designed to sell a product.

Bastion Brief was created to close that gap. We publish practical, plain-language guides about the threats people actually face — phishing, account takeover, scams, malware, privacy erosion — and the steps that genuinely reduce risk.

## What the name means

A **bastion** is the angular projection on a fortress wall, designed so that defenders could cover every approach and no wall was left unwatched. Good security works the same way: it is rarely one big wall, but a set of overlapping layers — a strong password, a second factor, a careful pause before clicking — each covering the gaps in the others.

Our logo is a shield split into two tones with a keyhole at its centre. The two halves stand for the two sides of every guide we write: understanding how an attack works, and knowing how to stop it. The keyhole is a reminder that access should only ever be granted, never taken.

A **brief** is what you give someone who needs to understand a situation quickly and act well. That is what every article aims to be.

## What we publish

- **Threat explainers** that describe how attacks work at the level a defender needs: enough to recognise them, not enough to carry them out.
- **Step-by-step tutorials** for securing accounts, devices and personal data.
- **Response guides** for when something has already gone wrong.
- **Ethical hacking and career explainers** that focus on authorised testing, the law, and legal ways to learn.

## What we will not publish

We will never publish instructions for gaining unauthorised access to accounts, devices or systems; for stealing credentials; for building or deploying malware; for running phishing campaigns; or for bypassing authentication. When readers search for those things, we answer the question behind the question: how the attack works, how to spot it, and how to stop it.

:::note Our educational purpose
Every article on Bastion Brief is published for defensive education. Understanding how threats operate is essential for protecting against them, but that understanding is offered for protection only. See our [disclaimer](/disclaimer/) for details.
:::

## How we work

Our articles are researched against primary sources wherever possible: official help centres from platforms such as Meta, Google and Apple; standards from bodies such as NIST and OWASP; and guidance from national agencies such as the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC). We do not invent statistics, testimonials or expert quotes. When we are not certain of something, we say so.

Platforms change their settings and processes frequently, so we review articles and update them when things change. Each article shows both its original publication date and the date of its most recent meaningful update. You can read more in our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/).

## Independence

Bastion Brief does not run advertising trackers and does not accept payment to recommend products. If that ever changes — for example through clearly labelled affiliate links — it will be disclosed on the page where it applies and in our editorial policy.
